HOW THE NUMBERS WORK

low half at limit. SHOW in the view switches the line colors between structural load, heat, or whichever is worse at each point.

Structural load is figured at 48 points down the body. Thrust pushes from the bottom, everything above has to be accelerated, and the air pushes sideways on the nose and fins when the rocket points away from the airflow. That makes compression and bending in the wall. Hot walls lose strength, so heat and load stack up.

Heat comes from air friction. The flow can only heat the skin toward the recovery temperature, which climbs with the square of Mach, while the heating rate grows with the cube of speed and the square root of air density. A sharp nose concentrates it, a blunt nose spreads it and slows down higher up. Hot skin sheds heat by radiating. The nose and fins tolerate about 1000 and 900 K, tank walls about 700 K.

Thrust follows the nozzle: thrust equals the vacuum thrust minus outside pressure times the exit area. A sea level bell loses a little at the pad, a vacuum bell loses most of it and the flow separates. The planet is round with real gravity, so going sideways fast enough means falling around it: about 7.8 km/s at 200 km.

Parachutes take damage when overloaded or opened too fast. Drogues are small and tough, mains are big and fragile. Each chute is rated for a force, so a dense body falling fast will tear any chute. Separate a light capsule if the whole rocket falls too fast.
